My favorite sci-fi book of all time. My dad told me for three or four years that I should read it and that I'd love it. Every time, I said "yeah, I'll read it," and then didn't. When I finally did, it took over.

The plot is so gripping that the meaning is built right into the fun parts. The things that keep you reading are the same things that get you thinking.

Like a lot of my favorites, it's humanity versus aliens. An alien force, the Buggers, is invading, and humanity trains children to fight them as soldiers and generals. Ender, the genius, is learning space combat and military tactics, building his own sense of morality, and figuring out how to command a team.

Meanwhile, down on Earth, his sadistic brother and his sweet, brilliant sister start publishing political and philosophical essays, become respected intellectuals, and take power in their own way. It's a great subplot.

Then at the end you learn that the simulations Ender has been running were real battles. A 12- or 13-year-old kid unknowingly wiped out billions of aliens.

The sequel, Speaker for the Dead, is beautiful too. Ender carries the last Bugger egg to a new world as humanity colonizes the galaxy, and the question becomes: we've seen how humanity brutalized the Buggers, so how will it treat this new, almost mystical pig species?

It's super fun to read, and it had me thinking about a lot: how we treat people we can't trust, how we govern the people we do know, and where morality fits when surviving a conflict pulls against being a good person. 10 out of 10.
