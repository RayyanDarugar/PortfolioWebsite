This is one of my favorite books I've ever read. The first few pages blew me away. It was one of the only times I've opened a book and been shocked by something that looks so simple on the surface. The words are plain, and I still remember thinking, "Oh, there's a lot going on here."

It opens with the main character as a kid, sitting and watching the stars. What she's thinking, and the way Carl Sagan writes it, hit me hard. I can't fully describe the feeling, but it was hopeful, it was curious, and it felt real at the same time. That's still one of my favorite parts of the book.

The premise is that aliens are talking to Earth. We have proof, and they're sending over plans for some kind of machine. But the book barely cares about the aliens. It's about how humanity reacts: religious groups, scientists, politicians, business leaders, and everyone else. You watch religion wrestle with "this isn't exactly what's in our book" and try to adapt. The relationships and the storyline are powerful.

What I liked most is the perspective on humanity it gave me. Sagan understood how the world thinks, and taking on that many points of view is hard. He communicates all of them beautifully.

It isn't a violent or action-heavy book. It's deeper and more memorable than that. It isn't just a sci-fi book, it's a portrait of humanity: the different groups and the different feelings, all tied together by the big question of what's going on in the universe, and why we have numbers like pi and the gravitational constant at all. It gripped me all the way to the end.
