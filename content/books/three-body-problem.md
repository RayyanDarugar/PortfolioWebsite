One of my current favorites. I first read it about a year ago, and I adore it. It matches all of my interests, and exploring the actual three-body world through the VR game is super interesting.

It's one of those books where you can read every word and understand it, but getting the feeling behind the words is something different. It's hard to describe. It feels like experiencing the words more than reading them, like there's always something deeper going on underneath. That feeling is really beautiful.

I sped through the whole book in a day or two because I was so in love with it. The plot asks what humanity does when there's an alien threat on the horizon: what factions form, and how people react. Cixin Liu is a great storyteller.

The sequels are some of the best books ever written. They keep mystifying me with scientific and pseudo-scientific takes on the human brain, nanomaterials, space elevators, and humanity's place in the galaxy. In The Dark Forest, Earth survives by figuring out that if any civilization finds another, the only safe move is to destroy it immediately, because trust is impossible. What does that mean for society? What does that mean for the galaxy?

Great themes, great writing, and in general just beautiful.
