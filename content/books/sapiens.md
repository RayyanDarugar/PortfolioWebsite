The best nonfiction book I've read outside of business books like How to Win Friends and Influence People, and the one that's had the most impact on how I think. Yuval Noah Harari covers the history of humanity: where we came from, what the earliest humans were like, and what the first civilizations looked like. He asks questions like whether we were happier before the agricultural revolution, and those tie into things I think about all the time.

Lately I've been thinking about the difference between morality, meaning, and purpose. Is there morality in a hedonistic life where you chase as much pleasure as possible, or do we have deeper responsibilities? What's the value of being kind? What does it mean to be good for yourself and for the people around you?

A lot of those questions come from living in a structured society, which started with the agricultural revolution. Would we be happier as small tribes living simply off the land instead of 8 billion people? Is there meaning in technological progress at all? This book is what got me asking.

It also taught me a lot of psychology, like how early tribes could only really know about 150 people, and how gossip was the way groups learned who to trust. And its take on what comes next, on intelligence and what it means to be sapient, is a great reminder of who humanity actually is in the first place, especially now that AI is the biggest thing in the world. Love this book.
